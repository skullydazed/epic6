# Flood control (removed incoming detector)

EPIC's former built-in flood-control facility limited repeated incoming messages reaching the display. EPIC6 has removed that detector, its tracking list, its settings, and the `FLOOD` event. The rules below describe the historical facility, not an active EPIC6 policy.

Client-side display suppression cannot stop incoming traffic from consuming bandwidth or the processing needed to receive and inspect it.

## Historical sender grouping

The former `FLOOD_MASKUSER` setting selected how senders were grouped:

| Value | Grouping |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Treat each unique `user@host` as a distinct potential flooder. |
| 1 | Track identd users separately, but combine non-identd users on the same host. |
| 2 | Combine all users on a host into one potential flooder. |

Floods were tracked separately by target, server, and message level. A sender's traffic on different channels or at different levels could therefore form distinct tracked floods. Servers were exempt from that detector.

## Historical tracking list and penalties

`FLOOD_USERS` limited the number of potential floods tracked. A larger table consumed more resources, while a small one could lose track of relevant senders sooner.

New activity introduced an entry, displacing the oldest eligible entry without penalty points. When an entry with penalties would otherwise be displaced, it instead lost a penalty point. A sender that stopped flooding could eventually lose its penalties and become eligible for removal.

After `FLOOD_AFTER` messages, the detector compared the message rate with `FLOOD_RATE / FLOOD_RATE_PER`. Further messages above the threshold added penalty points, keeping the troublesome entry tracked longer. The former `floodinfo()` function inspected the list.

## Historical warnings and suppression

`FLOOD_WARNING` enabled a local warning when flooding was detected. `FLOOD_IGNORE` selected whether the normal handling of detected flood messages was suppressed. The original page's reference to “SET IGNORE OFF” in this explanation was inconsistent with its `FLOOD_IGNORE` setting; the intended contrast was that turning flood suppression off allowed normal processing.

Those names no longer configure a built-in incoming detector in EPIC6. A loaded script can implement its own policy, but accepting an old setting name would not restore the removed C implementation. Current message hooks such as [`general_privmsg`](../on/general_privmsg.md) and [`general_notice`](../on/general_notice.md) are separate event interfaces.

## Other uses of flood protection

The supplied [`floodprot`](../scripts/floodprot.md) script queues outgoing IRC lines. It does not classify incoming senders or recreate the old tracking list.

`source/ctcp.c` also retains an outgoing CTCP reply-throttling branch, but its `NO_CTCP_FLOOD` setting is absent from current registration. The branch updates its timestamp only when dropping a reply, not after successful replies. Its presence therefore does not establish a working general reply-rate limit or a supported `/set no_ctcp_flood` interface. See [the removed setting](set_no_ctcp_flood.md).

Server-side flood policies are independent of these client facilities. Local display suppression and outgoing queues cannot guarantee that a server will accept traffic without penalties.

## History

The original page described flood control as part of ircII's longstanding behavior. Its DokuWiki revision identifier was `flood_control.txt,v 1.1 2007/03/02 02:32:04 jnelson Exp`. EPIC6's retirement notes record removal of the built-in facility.
